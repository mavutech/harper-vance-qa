# Harper Vance sales conversation kit

## Summary

Phase 2 preparation, October 29, 2026. Use this kit to qualify a professional NQ team, demonstrate the existing product, and agree on a useful evaluation. Messages are drafts, not sent or scheduled. This adds no free trial, discount, license, or access entitlement.

## Key points

- Start with teams that already research or trade NQ and have a person responsible for reviewing results or selecting research tools.
- A relevant title alone is insufficient. Verify the person's current role and the firm's NQ relevance using a dated public source before personalizing outreach.
- Keep Mavu's personal profile focused on his professional work. No profile rewrite or SavaPay content is required for this campaign.
- Ask about one real workflow. Do not lead with an accuracy percentage, an AI disclaimer, or a claim of superior results.
- Use existing access and sample-request routes. Do not promise that an unapproved prospect can open authenticated dashboard links.

## Recommendations

### Before each conversation

Confirm the person, company, current role, public source URL, source date, specific relevance, preferred channel, and prior contact status in a private sales record. Choose one initial contact per account. A second contact is a fallback, not a simultaneous message. Do not invent an email address or imply familiarity with work you have not reviewed.

Have the public walkthrough and corrected report available. Use the private full-period package only after its distribution is approved for that audience. Keep the dashboard sidebar, account email, customer lists, and credentials off screen. Do not record a prospect without consent.

### Twenty minute demonstration

| Minutes | Show or ask | Suggested wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 3 | Understand their current process | “How does your team review an NQ session today? What takes more work than it should?” |
| 3 to 6 | Dashboard target and context | “This is the dashboard. You can see the target, how long it's been open, and how its behavior compares with similar historical targets. The comparison is context, not a promise about this one.” |
| 6 to 10 | A complete daily record | “Did it get hit? How long did it take? What happened along the way? This is where you can review that, including the targets that weren't reached.” |
| 10 to 14 | Complete period and difficult examples | “I don't want to show you only a good day. Let's look at the full period, then pick a session you want to inspect.” Explain missing measurements and recalculated records before drawing conclusions |
| 14 to 17 | Their delivery and team requirements | “Would you use this in the dashboard, in your team's review, or through an integration?” Distinguish daily reports from live-target delivery |
| 17 to 20 | Decide the next step | “What would you need to check to decide whether this is useful enough to pay for?” Agree one task, owner, and follow-up date, or a clear no-fit decision |

Do not claim original open-target context for a historical record when it was not preserved. A separately dated recorded example can demonstrate the interface, not prove that past context or live customer receipt.

### First contact drafts

Replace bracketed fields with verified details. Do not send unresolved placeholders. These drafts use the approved founder voice and title.

Connection note, where a note is available:

> Hi [Name], I saw [specific verified NQ-related detail]. I'm the technical founder of Harper Vance. We work on live NQ targets and the context around them. I'd be glad to connect.

First substantive message after connection, or as an authorized direct message:

> Hi [Name], [one short, verified reason their work is relevant].
>
> I'm the technical founder of Harper Vance. It brings live NQ targets together with context and a record of what happened afterward, including misses.
>
> I'm interested in how your team reviews that today. Would a short walkthrough be useful?

If no individual detail is available but the firm's NQ relevance is verified, say that plainly. Do not fabricate a compliment, recent post, mutual connection, or customer referral.

### Follow up drafts

First follow-up, after five business days without a reply:

> Hi [Name], one thing the walkthrough covers is what happens between a target appearing and getting reached. Time open and pullback can matter as much as the final outcome. If that's part of your team's review, I'm happy to show you.

Final follow-up, five business days later if there is still no reply:

> Hi [Name], I'll leave this here. If reviewing NQ target behavior becomes a priority, I'd be happy to walk you through Harper Vance.

Stop after that. Stop immediately for an objection, opt-out, wrong-person response, or ongoing conversation. Do not send the same sequence by email and LinkedIn in parallel. If an invitation is not accepted, do not treat these as authorization to circumvent that through another channel.

After a real demonstration:

> Thanks, [Name]. You mentioned [their actual workflow problem]. The part of Harper Vance that seems most relevant is [specific demonstrated capability].
>
> We agreed to check [observable task] and review it on [agreed date]. I'll provide [approved material or access only]. If that doesn't match what you had in mind, let me know.

Use “Mavu Kasende, Technical Founder” where a signature is appropriate. Do not add a signature mechanically to every LinkedIn reply.

### Competitive and price conversation

> If your current tools already handle this well, I wouldn't ask you to replace them just to add another platform. The question is whether our NQ target context and review workflow fill a gap for your team. Let's compare that against what you actually need.

Compare the buyer's required workflow, sample quality, internal usage rights, delivery needs, setup effort, and actual price. Do not compare an individual entry subscription against a team arrangement as though the scope were identical. Do not assert competitor prices or shortcomings without a fresh primary-source check. Do not discount before understanding the objection.

### Evaluation agreement checklist

Record the buyer's existing process, one task to evaluate, agreed start/end dates, authorized participants, material/access scope, current applicable plan, success criteria, decision-maker, and review appointment. Criteria may include finding a complete session record, interpreting a miss correctly, identifying a useful contextual measure, or completing an authorized delivery check. These are proposed checks, not customer results.

Measure any claimed time saving against the buyer's own comparable task before and after use. Do not assign a percentage improvement in advance. No free access or trial duration is offered by this checklist.

## Risks with mitigations

- Overpromising: never substitute a historical hit rate for a buying case or expected profit.
- Generic cold messaging: use one verified relevance detail, one product point, and one invitation.
- Too much contact: one account owner, one active channel, bounded follow-ups, immediate stop on opt-out.
- Weak qualification: separate professional NQ relevance, demonstrated need, buying authority, and budget rather than scoring a title alone.
- Unapproved terms: use current approved commercial materials; escalate any requested discount, new license, or unsupported integration.
