# First five client evaluation workflow

## Summary

Phase 4 operating materials are ready. No prospect has been contacted or evaluated by this work. Use a private sales record for actual people and results. Blank fields below are templates, not evidence of active opportunities.

## Key points

- One record per account and one responsible contact at a time.
- Record a specific reason for fit, not simply “finance” or “trading.”
- An evaluation begins only when the buyer agrees on scope, participants, criteria, and a review date.
- Count a client only when the approved commercial agreement is completed, the first payment is confirmed, and authorized access is provisioned. Track each condition separately; reconcile exceptions with the owner.
- Keep the existing [measurement definitions](../landing-page/sales-feedback-playbook.md). Clicks, invitations, meetings, evaluations, and paid clients are different stages.

### Evaluation worksheet

| Question | Before evaluation | After evaluation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What task are we trying to improve? | Buyer's description | Same task, not a substitute |
| How is it handled now? | Actual baseline workflow | What changed, if anything |
| Can the buyer find and interpret the full record? | Agreed check | Observed completion or difficulty |
| Can they distinguish resolution from return? | Ask without coaching | Record their explanation |
| Is target context useful for their task? | Expected use, labeled as expectation | Actual example or “not useful” |
| Are delivery and access sufficient? | Requirements | Verified fit or outstanding technical check |
| What does the buyer want to do next? | Decision process | Buy, continue with a reason/date, decline, or defer |

## Recommendations

### Proposed first execution cycle

These dates are a manual work plan, not scheduled messages or promised meetings. If distribution and recipient approval are not ready, shift the contact dates rather than bypass the gate.

| Date | Owner action | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friday October 30, 2026 | Review the private package; select five accounts from a freshly verified list | Approved material and five account records, or specific corrections |
| Monday November 2 | Confirm role, relevance and contact history; approve five individualized messages | Ready-to-send batch, one contact per account |
| Tuesday November 3 | Send the approved first batch only if separately authorized | Actual sent records, not assumed delivery or replies |
| Wednesday November 4 | Respond to genuine replies and qualify interested buyers | Agreed demo times or clear no-fit records |
| Thursday November 5 | Hold any agreed demonstrations; otherwise continue research and reply handling | Actual notes, not invented appointments |
| Friday November 6 | Review batch results and repeated objections | Counts with denominators; one evidence-backed improvement |
| Tuesday November 10 | First follow-up for unanswered November 3 messages, only where appropriate | Actual follow-up records; skip replies and opt-outs |
| Tuesday November 17 | Final follow-up for still-unanswered eligible messages | Close the cold sequence afterward |

Dates are operating suggestions, not claims of optimal send-time performance. Do not automate LinkedIn account actions or purchase contact lists under this preparation scope.

### Weekly review

Record contacted accounts, replies, positive replies, meetings held, evaluations agreed, completed evaluations, buying decisions, and paid/activated clients for the same cohort and period. Record opt-outs and delivery problems. For each rate, retain its numerator and denominator. With five accounts, discuss actual counts and objections, not statistically proven conversion changes.

If no one replies, inspect fit and message relevance before changing price. If meetings happen but evaluations do not, examine usefulness and clarity. If evaluations happen but purchases do not, document the actual reason: evidence, budget, rights, setup, trust, or fit. Do not assume every objection needs a product feature.

## Risks with mitigations

- Empty funnel disguised as progress: templates stay blank until actual activity occurs.
- Too many speculative prospects: approve a small researched cohort before scaling.
- Unbounded evaluations: agree a task, reviewer and decision date without inventing a free trial.
- Privacy: actual contact details, notes and buyer statements belong in the private sales system.
- Overcounting success: a positive reply or verbal interest is not a paid client.
