# Customer evidence and publication checklist

## Summary

Phase 5 collection and review materials are ready. No customer outcome, quotation, case study, or testimonial is claimed. Publish only after real use, evidence review, and permission for the exact material and destination.

## Key points

- Useful evidence may describe a better review process, clearer records, or a working integration. It does not have to claim improved trading returns.
- Separate what the buyer says from what records demonstrate.
- Preserve neutral and negative feedback. An anonymized story still needs appropriate permission and must not reveal the customer indirectly.
- Do not turn the September historical evaluation into a customer success story.

## Table

| Evidence | Collect | Required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workflow use | Actual task, participants' roles, dates, frequency | Confirm the customer really used the demonstrated capability |
| Time saving | Comparable task, baseline, observed times, method, sample count | Report measured scope and limitations; do not extrapolate a single task into company-wide savings |
| Integration reliability | Approved event window, expected events, accepted events, receiver-confirmed events, failures | Distinguish HTTP acceptance, receiver receipt, and downstream completion |
| Buyer quotation | Exact words and surrounding context | Written approval of exact quote, attribution, and publication destination |
| Customer logo/name | Named approver and permitted use | Explicit permission; customer relationship alone is not permission |
| Negative result | Problem, scope, severity, impact, next action | Do not silently remove it from the evaluation record |

## Recommendations

### Adoption review questions

Ask after the buyer has had enough agreed use to answer, not merely after payment:

1. What did you actually use Harper Vance for?
2. Which part was useful? Can you show a specific example?
3. What remained difficult or did not help?
4. Did it change your process? What records could support that?
5. What would you need to keep using it?
6. Would you consider approving a short account of that experience after reviewing the exact wording?

### Publication decision

Keep a case study on hold if a claim cannot be supported, the customer has not approved the final wording, the intended audience is outside the permission, or confidential details remain. New website sections, social publication, and use of customer marks need their own approval. This checklist grants none of those permissions.

For competitive positioning, ask which alternatives the buyer actually considered and why they chose or rejected Harper Vance. Quote only what they authorize. Do not convert one customer's preference into proof of general superiority.

## Risks with mitigations

- Inflated benefits: use the narrow result the evidence supports.
- Misleading quotations: preserve meaning and obtain approval after edits.
- Identifiable anonymous customer: remove unique details and review with the customer.
- Unmeasured reliability: retain the period and expected-event denominator; report missing observations.
- Premature completion: a ready worksheet is not a completed customer-evidence phase.
